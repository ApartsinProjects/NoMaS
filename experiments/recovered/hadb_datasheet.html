01

Filter funnel

Every candidate dataset is screened by the same pipeline. A dataset survives only if enough of its anomalies resist a trivial rule, it is not degenerate or over-dense, some detector beats the base rate (not floor), and detectors actually spread apart (selection matters). Bars show, per corpus, the fraction included versus screened out.

02

Composition & provenance

Two modalities across six public corpora. Tabular triviality is measured per anomaly (share dropped); time-series triviality is measured per series (share of whole series solvable by a one-liner). Dev corpora carry the classic labelled collections; test corpora are held aside.

03

Difficulty distributions

Box = interquartile range, line = median, whiskers = min/max, over the 307 included datasets. best_ap_norm is how well the single best detector does; spread is the gap between best and mean; both are the levers a selection method is judged on.

05

Adversarial validity checks

Each check states its expected outcome before running, so a silent defect shows up as a violated invariant. All pass on the consolidated benchmark.

06

Known characteristics

A
Base-rate regimes differ by modality. Tabular datasets pile at the 0.25 test-rate cap (q3 = 0.250); time series sit much lower (median 0.066). The primary metric ap_norm normalises for base rate, and its near-zero correlation with difficulty (r = 0.06) confirms the normalisation holds across both regimes.
B
Pool size tracks modality, not difficulty. Time-series arms rank 23 candidates, tabular arms 35–36. That is why base_rate and n_variants correlate (r = 0.56): n_variants is a per-arm pool marker, not a difficulty lever. A selector ranks whatever a dataset lists.
C
best_ap_norm and best_auc are redundant (r = 0.86). Both answer "how good is the best detector". ap_norm is the single primary; AUC and pAUC@10 are retained only as secondaries.
D
Local detectors lead the winner mix. LOF variants take the largest share, though 34 detectors win somewhere and the global family still claims 40% of datasets. The candidate pool carries six LOF configurations, so LOF prominence partly reflects pool composition.
